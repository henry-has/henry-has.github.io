How it works

S is how many groups are wired in series (sets voltage). P is how many cells are in each group, side by side (sets capacity and current). 7S3P = 7 groups of 3 cells.

Fill group S1, then S2, and so on. Each group must touch the group before it, so current can flow from PACK − to PACK +. A group can be one cluster or split into pieces; each extra piece costs that group 10%, which can be worth it if it evens out the current.

Your result is the effective resistance in mΩ, lower is better: the pack's resistance, scaled up by how overloaded the hardest-working cell is and by any split groups. Par is the best layout the bot found.

Touching cells are joined by a strip. Every strip is 1 mΩ and every cell is 10 mΩ.

Groups alternate direction, so a group's strips to the next group are on one face and its strips to the previous group are on the other. Both faces are shown.
